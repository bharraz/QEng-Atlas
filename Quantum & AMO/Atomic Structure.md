#AMO

**Hydrogen sets the scales, quantum numbers label the ladder, and each finer level of structure is a power of $\alpha$ down.** Gross structure (eV) → fine structure ($\alpha^2 \sim 10^{-4}$ smaller) → hyperfine (another $\sim m_e/m_p$ down).

# Reference

**Hydrogen scales:**

$$E_n = -\frac{\mathrm{Ry}}{n^2}, \qquad \mathrm{Ry} = 13.6\ \mathrm{eV}, \qquad a_0 = 0.529\ \text{Å}$$

Electron velocity $\sim \alpha c$ with $\alpha \approx 1/137$ — the expansion parameter for everything relativistic.

**Quantum numbers:** $n$ (energy shell), $l < n$ (orbital), $s = \tfrac{1}{2}$, and once spin–orbit coupling acts, $j = |l - s|, \dots, l + s$ with $m_j$. $l$ and $s$ separately stop being good; $j$ survives.

**Fine structure** = relativistic kinetic correction + spin–orbit $\xi(r)\,\mathbf{L}\cdot\mathbf{S}$ (+ Darwin for $l=0$), all of order

$$\Delta E_{\mathrm{fs}} \sim \alpha^2\, \mathrm{Ry} \sim 10\ \mathrm{GHz\ scale}$$

splitting each $l > 0$ level into $j = l \pm \tfrac{1}{2}$. This is the $P_{1/2}/P_{3/2}$ doublet you tune your lasers between (Ca⁺ 397/393 nm, Yb⁺ 369/329 nm).

**Term symbol decoder** $^{2S+1}L_J$: superscript = spin multiplicity, letter = total $L$ (S, P, D, F...), subscript = $J$. So $^2S_{1/2}$: one valence electron, $L=0$, $J=\tfrac{1}{2}$ — the ground state of every alkali and alkali-like ion. Single-valence ions (Ca⁺, Sr⁺, Yb⁺, Ba⁺) are hydrogen-with-a-quantum-defect: same labels, shifted energies, plus low-lying metastable $D$ states that hydrogen lacks — the clock/shelving levels.

> [!question]- Why does fine structure scale as $\alpha^2$ relative to gross structure?
> The electron moves at $v \sim \alpha c$, so relativistic corrections (and the motional magnetic field driving spin–orbit) enter at $(v/c)^2 = \alpha^2$. Hence heavier atoms (larger effective $Z$, $\Delta E \propto Z^4$-ish) have far bigger fine structure.
