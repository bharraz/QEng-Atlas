**The Magnus expansion writes time evolution as a single exponential $U(t) = e^{\Omega(t)}$ with $\Omega$ built from nested commutators of $H$ at different times — so every truncation stays in the group and is exactly unitary.**

You are approximating the *generator*, not the propagator; the side-by-side with Dyson is on [[Propagators, Time Ordering, and the Dyson Series]].

# Reference

For $i\dot{U} = H(t)U$ (with $\hbar = 1$), write $U(t) = \exp\!\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \Omega_k(t)\right]$ with

$$\Omega_1 = -i\int_0^t \! dt_1\, H(t_1)$$

$$\Omega_2 = -\frac{1}{2}\int_0^t \! dt_1 \int_0^{t_1}\! dt_2\, [H(t_1), H(t_2)]$$

$$\Omega_3 = \frac{i}{6}\int_0^t\!\! \int_0^{t_1}\!\! \int_0^{t_2} \big( [H_1,[H_2,H_3]] + [H_3,[H_2,H_1]] \big)\, dt_3\, dt_2\, dt_1$$

with $H_i \equiv H(t_i)$. Each $\Omega_k$ is a $k$-fold time-ordered integral of $(k{-}1)$-nested commutators — equivalently a fixed combination of length-$k$ bracket-words, read as a $k$-linear map awaiting its arguments ([[Multilinear Maps and Polarization]], [[Free Algebras, Words, and Gradings]]). Key structural facts:

- **Every $\Omega_k$ is anti-Hermitian**, so any truncation $e^{\Omega_1 + \dots + \Omega_n}$ is exactly unitary. The Dyson series truncated at any order is not.
- **$\Omega_k$ lives in the Lie algebra** generated by $\{H(t)\}$ — the expansion never leaves the algebra, so symmetries of $H$ are preserved order by order (see [[Lie Algebras (Atlas)]]).
- If $[H(t_1), H(t_2)] = 0$ for all times, the series terminates at $\Omega_1$: $U = e^{-i\int H\,dt}$, the naive guess. All higher terms are the price of non-commutativity at different times — the same origin as [[Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff]].
- **Convergence** is guaranteed for $\int_0^t \lVert H(t')\rVert\, dt' < \pi$ (**Moan–Niesen** bound). In practice: short times or weak/fast drives.

**Picture:** a long walk on a curved surface replaced by one equivalent straight step, corrected order by order for how successive moves fail to commute.

**When it terminates exactly.** Termination is an algebraic property, not a small-parameter one — it happens iff the algebra generated by $\{H(t)\}$ is **ad-nilpotent**, so that nested brackets vanish at finite depth ([[Conjugation]], [[Subalgebras, Ideals, and Quotients]]).

| Generated algebra | Terminates at | Why |
|---|---|---|
| abelian, $[H(t_1),H(t_2)] = 0$ | $\Omega_1$ | naive $e^{-i\int H dt}$; pulse area only |
| $\mathfrak h_3 = \{a, a^\dagger, \mathbb 1\}$, i.e. $H$ **linear** in $\hat x, \hat p$ | $\Omega_2$ | $[H(t_1),H(t_2)]$ is a c-number, central, so $\Omega_{k\geq3} = 0$ |
| $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, $\mathfrak{su}(1,1)$, quadratic | never terminates, but **closes** | finite $\dim\mathfrak g$ ⇒ solve by Wei–Norman instead ([[Solving Dynamics on Closed Lie Algebras]]) |
| generic, unbounded | never | truncate and bound the error |

The $\Omega_2$ row is the trapped-ion case and the reason the [[Molmer-Sorensen Gate]] has a closed form at any drive strength and for any pulse envelope: $\Omega_1$ is a spin-dependent displacement (closing for a full phase-space loop) and $\Omega_2$ is the geometric phase — the enclosed area — that becomes the entangling gate. Adding a term quadratic in $\hat a, \hat a^\dagger$ leaves $\mathfrak h_3$ and destroys the termination ([[Weyl Algebra and Degree Filtration]]).

**Average Hamiltonian theory (AHT).** For a periodic $H(t)$ with cycle time $T$, define the effective Hamiltonian by $U(T) = e^{-i\bar{H}T}$ and expand $\bar{H} = \bar{H}^{(0)} + \bar{H}^{(1)} + \dots$:

$$\bar{H}^{(0)} = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T \tilde{H}(t)\, dt, \qquad \bar{H}^{(1)} = -\frac{i}{2T}\int_0^T\!\! dt_1 \int_0^{t_1}\!\! dt_2\, [\tilde{H}(t_1), \tilde{H}(t_2)],$$

where $\tilde{H}$ is the Hamiltonian in the **toggling frame** (the interaction frame of the pulses). Pulse-sequence design is the art of choosing pulses so the unwanted terms of $\bar{H}^{(0)}$ average to zero, then symmetrizing the sequence in time so $\bar{H}^{(1)}$ (all odd orders, in fact) vanish too. [[Spin Echo and Dynamical Decoupling]] sequences (CPMG, XY-8, WAHUHA) are exactly this: the echo cancels the static term at $\bar{H}^{(0)}$; higher-order sequences chase down the residual commutator terms.

**Floquet connection**: for periodic driving, $\bar{H}$ is the Floquet Hamiltonian, and the Magnus series in $1/\omega_{\text{drive}}$ is the standard high-frequency expansion.

> [!question]- Why prefer Magnus over the Dyson series when both are expansions of the same $U(t)$?
> Truncated Dyson violates unitarity — probabilities leak and fake decay/gain appears at long times. Truncated Magnus errs only *within* the group: you get a wrong but perfectly unitary evolution, with symmetries intact. For designing control sequences this is the correct error model: the residual is an effective Hamiltonian you can attack at the next order, not a non-physical loss.
