#math

**The Y_lm are the eigenfunctions of the angular part of the Laplacian — the only shapes angular structure can take on a sphere** — which is why the same functions label atomic orbitals, multipole moments, and radiation patterns.

# Reference

$$
\nabla^2 = \frac{1}{r^2}\partial_r(r^2\partial_r) - \frac{\hat{L}^2}{\hbar^2 r^2}, \qquad \hat{L}^2\, Y_{lm} = \hbar^2\, l(l+1)\, Y_{lm}, \quad \hat{L}_z Y_{lm} = \hbar m\, Y_{lm}
$$
Structure: $Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi) \propto P_l^m(\cos\theta)\, e^{im\phi}$, with $l = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ and $|m| \leq l$ ($2l+1$ values).

**Legendre polynomials** ($m=0$, axially symmetric case): $P_0 = 1$, $P_1 = \cos\theta$, $P_2 = \frac{1}{2}(3\cos^2\theta - 1)$. Generating function = the multipole expansion:
$$
\frac{1}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|} = \sum_l \frac{r'^l}{r^{l+1}}\, P_l(\cos\gamma)
$$

**First few $Y_{lm}$:**
$$
Y_{00} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}, \qquad Y_{10} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\cos\theta, \qquad Y_{1,\pm1} = \mp\sqrt{\frac{3}{8\pi}}\sin\theta\, e^{\pm i\phi}
$$
$Y_{20} = \sqrt{5/16\pi}\,(3\cos^2\theta - 1)$. These are s, p, d angular shapes verbatim.

**Orthonormality and completeness** on the sphere:
$$
\int Y_{l'm'}^*\, Y_{lm}\, d\Omega = \delta_{ll'}\delta_{mm'}, \qquad f(\theta,\phi) = \sum_{lm} c_{lm} Y_{lm}
$$
— Fourier analysis on the sphere; $c_{lm}$ extracted by projection as always.

**Physics:** $l$ counts angular nodes and sets the centrifugal barrier $l(l+1)/r^2$; multipole moments are the $c_{lm}$ of a charge distribution; dipole radiation is $l=1$; selection rules ($\Delta l = \pm 1$ for E1) are statements about integrals of three $Y_{lm}$'s.

> [!question]- Why exactly $2l+1$ values of m, and what breaks their degeneracy?
> $m$ is the eigenvalue of $L_z$, bounded by $|m| \le l$ since $\langle L_z^2\rangle \le \langle L^2\rangle$ — giving $-l \ldots l$. They're degenerate because no direction is special; a magnetic field picks one and splits them (Zeeman).
