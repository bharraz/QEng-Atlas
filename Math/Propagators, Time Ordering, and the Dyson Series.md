#quantum #math

**The propagator is the solution operator of a linear evolution equation — and when the generator fails to commute with itself at different times, $\exp\int A\,dt$ is wrong, with Dyson and Magnus the two systematic repairs.**

# Reference

For $\dot\psi = A(t)\,\psi$ , Schrödinger with $A = -iH/\hbar$, a classical linear system with $A$ the state matrix, an open system with $A = \mathcal{L}$:

|            | Object                                     | Lives in                        | Composes by |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Generator  | $A(t)$, instantaneous rate                 | Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$       | addition    |
| Propagator | $U(t,t_0)$, with $\psi(t) = U\psi(t_0)$    | Lie group $G$                   | multiplication |

**Picture:** the propagator carries any initial state to its later state; when the generator changes with time, it is a product of many short-time propagators in time order.

$\partial_t U = A(t)\,U$, $U(t_0,t_0) = \mathbb 1$, $U(t_2,t_0) = U(t_2,t_1)U(t_1,t_0)$. Generators add, propagators multiply; every scheme below is a choice of which of the two to approximate.

**Time-independent case:** $U = e^{A(t-t_0)}$ — the [[Matrix Exponential]], $\sum_n e^{-iE_n(t-t_0)/\hbar}|n\rangle\langle n|$ in the eigenbasis. Diagonalizing *is* solving the dynamics.

**Time-dependent case:** $U = \exp\!\big[\int_{t_0}^t A\,dt'\big]$ is exact **iff** $[A(t_1), A(t_2)] = 0$ for all pairs of times.

- Self-commuting: time dependence is a *scalar* $f(t)$ multiplying a fixed operator. Only the pulse area matters, not the shape.
- Not self-commuting: any drive whose *axis* moves — rotating field, chirp, bichromatic force, [[Interaction Picture]] Hamiltonians with several detunings.

**Dyson series** — iterate $U = \mathbb 1 + \int A U$:
$$U(t,t_0) = \mathbb 1 + \int_{t_0}^{t}\!\! dt_1\, A(t_1) + \int_{t_0}^{t}\!\! dt_1\!\! \int_{t_0}^{t_1}\!\! dt_2\, A(t_1)A(t_2) + \cdots \;\equiv\; \mathcal T \exp\!\left[\int_{t_0}^t A\,dt'\right]$$
Nested limits enforce $t_1 > t_2 > \cdots$: later generators stand to the left. $\mathcal T$ is *notation for this series*, not an ordinary exponential. With $A = -iH_I/\hbar$ the $n$-th term is $n$-th order [[Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory]]; first order gives [[Fermi's Golden Rule]].

- **Truncation leaves the group.** A polynomial in $A$ is not a group element: truncated Dyson is not unitary, so probability leaks.
- **Secular error.** Terms carry powers of $t$, so any truncation fails at long times however weak the drive.
- Right tool for perturbative short-time transition amplitudes; wrong tool for long-time evolution.

**Fubini's theorem is the bridge to Magnus.** The second Dyson term integrates over the triangle $t_1 > t_2$. Split the full square into its two triangles and relabel the second by Fubini ($t_1 \leftrightarrow t_2$):

$$\left(\int A\right)^{2} = \iint_{t_1>t_2} \big\{A(t_1)A(t_2) + A(t_2)A(t_1)\big\}$$

so the triangle integral splits into a symmetric half plus a commutator:

$$\underbrace{\iint_{t_1>t_2}\!\! A(t_1)A(t_2)}_{\text{Dyson}_2} = \tfrac12\left(\int A\right)^{2} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 \iint_{t_1>t_2}\!\! [A(t_1), A(t_2)]}_{\Omega_2}$$

The symmetric half is what $e^{\Omega_1}$ already supplies; the leftover commutator is exactly Magnus's $\Omega_2$. **Swapping integration order is the step that converts time ordering into commutators** — the same move at every order.

**Magnus** — write $U = e^{\Omega(t)}$ and expand the *generator*: $\Omega_1 = \int A$, $\Omega_2$ as above, higher terms nested commutators ([[Magnus Expansion]]). Every truncation is $\exp$ of an algebra element, hence exactly in the group: unitarity, symplecticity and trace preservation all survive truncation. If the algebra generated by $\{A(t)\}$ is small the series closes or terminates ([[Dynamical Lie Algebras and Closure]]).

|                          | Dyson                                    | Magnus                                     |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Expands                  | propagator $U$                           | generator $\Omega = \log U$                |
| Truncation lives in      | nothing in particular                    | the Lie group                              |
| Unitary at finite order  | no                                       | yes                                        |
| Natural regime           | weak coupling, short times, rates        | strong/fast drives, long times, control    |
| Convergence              | coupling strength                        | $\int_0^T \lVert A\rVert\,dt < \pi$ (Moan–Niesen) |

**Same dichotomy elsewhere:** [[Trotter Product Formula|Trotterization]] approximates a group element by group elements (product form, also structure-preserving; Trotter–Kato extends the limit to unbounded generators and is the digital↔continuous bridge); Runge–Kutta approximates the state polynomially, hence Dyson-like norm leakage in [[Solving ODEs Numerically|quantum integrators]]; average Hamiltonian theory is Magnus at the sequence period.

> [!question]- The interaction-picture Hamiltonian self-commutes at all times. What do Dyson and Magnus reduce to?
> Time ordering is vacuous. Dyson's nested integrals un-nest into powers of $\int H_I$ and resum to $e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int H_I dt}$; Magnus has $\Omega_2 = \Omega_3 = \cdots = 0$. Pulse *area* sets the rotation, pulse shape is irrelevant.
