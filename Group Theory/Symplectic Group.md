#group-theory #math

**Linear transformations of positions and momenta that preserve phase-space area — rotations in phase space, squeezing, beam-splitter mixing of modes — form the symplectic group $Sp(2n,\mathbb R)$: the group preserving the symplectic form, simultaneously the canonical transformations of classical mechanics and the Gaussian unitaries of quantum mechanics, because the canonical commutation relations *are* that form with operator entries.** **In practice:** the dynamics of any Hamiltonian quadratic in $x, p$ (or $a, a^\dagger$) is a $2n\times 2n$ real matrix, and a Gaussian state is fixed by a covariance matrix whose *symplectic* eigenvalues, not its ordinary ones, decide whether it is physical.

# Reference

## Definition

With $z = (q_1,\dots,q_n,p_1,\dots,p_n)^{\mathsf T}$ and
$$J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \mathbb{1}_n \\ -\mathbb{1}_n & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad J^2 = -\mathbb 1, \qquad J^{\mathsf T} = J^{-1} = -J,$$
$$Sp(2n,\mathbb{R}) = \{ M \in \mathbb{R}^{2n\times 2n} : M^{\mathsf T} J M = J \}.$$

The preserved object is $\omega(u,v) = u^{\mathsf T}Jv$ — the sum of signed areas of the $(q_i,p_i)$-plane projections of the parallelogram $(u,v)$ ([[Bilinear Forms and Preserved Structures]]).

**Picture:** phase space as an incompressible fluid: symplectic maps can stretch and shear a patch of states but never change its area.

**Block form.** For $M = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}$, the condition $M^{\mathsf T}JM = J$ reads: $A^{\mathsf T}C$ and $B^{\mathsf T}D$ symmetric, $A^{\mathsf T}D - C^{\mathsf T}B = \mathbb 1$. For $n=1$ it collapses to $\det M = 1$, so $Sp(2,\mathbb R) = SL(2,\mathbb R)$; for $n \geq 2$ symplectic is strictly stronger than $\det M = 1$.

## Algebraic properties

- **Inverse:** $M^{-1} = -J M^{\mathsf T} J$ — the analog of $O^{-1} = O^{\mathsf T}$; inversion needs only a transpose and multiplication by $J$.
- **Determinant:** $\det M = +1$ for every symplectic $M$, not merely $\pm 1$. Liouville's theorem follows, but is weaker: *every* projected area sum is preserved, not only total volume.
- **Lie algebra:** differentiating $e^{tX^{\mathsf T}} J e^{tX} = J$ gives $X^{\mathsf T}J + JX = 0$, i.e. $X = JS$ with $S = S^{\mathsf T}$. Generators are in bijection with symmetric matrices, i.e. with **quadratic Hamiltonians** $H = \tfrac12 z^{\mathsf T}Sz$: Hamilton's equations $\dot z = J\nabla_z H = JSz$. $\dim Sp(2n,\mathbb R) = n(2n+1)$.
- **Spectrum:** eigenvalues come in quadruples $\lambda,\, 1/\lambda,\, \bar\lambda,\, 1/\bar\lambda$. So they either lie on the unit circle (stable oscillation) or occur in growth–decay pairs off it; a Floquet multiplier crossing $\pm 1$ is the onset of parametric instability.
- **Non-compact:** squeezing parameters are unbounded, so there is no invariant averaging and no faithful finite-dimensional unitary representation — on Hilbert space the group acts through the infinite-dimensional metaplectic representation. Squeezing has no periodicity, unlike rotation.

**The single-mode algebra in oscillator language.** $\mathfrak{sp}(2,\mathbb R) \cong \mathfrak{su}(1,1)$ is spanned by
$$K_+ = \tfrac12 a^{\dagger 2}, \qquad K_- = \tfrac12 a^{2}, \qquad K_0 = \tfrac12\!\left(a^\dagger a + \tfrac12\right)$$
with $[K_+, K_-] = -2K_0$, $[K_0, K_\pm] = \pm K_\pm$. **This is where the anomalous $a^2, a^{\dagger 2}$ terms live**, and it closes — which is why any Hamiltonian quadratic in $(a, a^\dagger)$ is exactly solvable however it depends on time ([[Solving Dynamics on Closed Lie Algebras]]). Adding $\{a, a^\dagger, \mathbb 1\}$ gives the 6-dimensional algebra of all quadratic-plus-linear Hamiltonians; adding a *cubic* term destroys closure.

## Decompositions

**Euler / Bloch–Messiah** — every $M \in Sp(2n,\mathbb R)$ factors as
$$M = K_1\, \Sigma\, K_2,$$
with $K_1, K_2 \in Sp(2n,\mathbb R)\cap O(2n) \cong U(n)$ the maximal compact subgroup and $\Sigma = \mathrm{diag}(e^{r_1},\dots,e^{r_n},e^{-r_1},\dots,e^{-r_n})$. Optically: $K_1, K_2$ are passive beamsplitter/phase-shifter networks and $\Sigma$ is a bank of single-mode squeezers. **Any Gaussian transformation = passive optics, single-mode squeezing, passive optics.**

**Williamson** — for any real symmetric positive-definite $\sigma$ there is $M \in Sp(2n,\mathbb R)$ with
$$M \sigma M^{\mathsf T} = \mathrm{diag}(\nu_1, \dots, \nu_n, \nu_1, \dots, \nu_n), \qquad \nu_k > 0.$$
The $\nu_k$ are the **symplectic eigenvalues**, computed as ordinary eigenvalues of $|iJ\sigma|$. They are invariant under symplectic congruence; the ordinary eigenvalues of $\sigma$ are not.

## Quantum mechanics

$[z_i, z_j] = i\hbar J_{ij}$: a linear map $z \to Mz$ of mode operators preserves the commutators iff $M$ is symplectic, and the unitaries implementing such maps are exactly those generated by quadratic Hamiltonians — the Gaussian unitaries.

- **Gaussian states reduce to matrix algebra.** First moments $\bar z \to M\bar z$, covariance by congruence $\sigma \to M\sigma M^{\mathsf T}$ — $2n$ dimensions regardless of Fock-space size.
- **Uncertainty principle:** $\sigma + \tfrac{i\hbar}{2}J \geq 0$, equivalently $\nu_k \geq \hbar/2$ for every symplectic eigenvalue. Purity, von Neumann entropy and Gaussian entanglement measures are functions of the $\nu_k$ alone.
- **Bogoliubov transformations** of [[Squeezed States]] are symplectic matrices on $(a, a^\dagger)$; two-mode squeezing, beamsplitters and phase shifts exhaust $Sp(4,\mathbb R)$.

## Classical mechanics and optics

- Canonical transformations are symplectic at the linearized level; the flow map of any Hamiltonian system is symplectic at all times.
- **Paraxial optics is the $n=1$ case:** [[Ray Transfer Matrices|ABCD matrices]] with unit determinant form $Sp(2,\mathbb R)$, and the conserved area underlies étendue invariance and $w_0\theta = \lambda/\pi$.
- **Normal-mode diagonalization** of a quadratic mechanical Hamiltonian is a symplectic congruence — Williamson applied classically ([[Normal Modes of Ion Chains]]).
- **Symplectic integrators** are those whose one-step map satisfies $M^{\mathsf T}JM = J$; that membership is what bounds energy error over long times ([[Solving ODEs Numerically]]).

> [!question]- A two-mode covariance matrix has ordinary eigenvalues well above $\hbar/2$, yet the state is unphysical. How?
> Physicality constrains the *symplectic* eigenvalues, not the ordinary ones. The two spectra differ whenever $\sigma$ is not already in Williamson form, and inter-mode correlations can push a $\nu_k$ below $\hbar/2$ while every ordinary eigenvalue stays large. Always check eigenvalues of $|iJ\sigma|$.
