#quantum-info

**The Clifford group is the set of unitaries that map Paulis to Paulis under conjugation** ($UPU^\dagger \in \mathcal{P}_n$) — big enough to build all of QEC, teleportation, and maximal entanglement, yet efficiently classically simulable.

# Reference

Generated by $H$, $S$, CNOT. Track states by conjugating their stabilizers:

| Gate | Conjugation action |
|---|---|
| $H$ | $X \leftrightarrow Z$, $Y \to -Y$ |
| $S$ | $X \to Y$, $Z \to Z$ |
| CNOT | $X_c \to X_c X_t$, $Z_t \to Z_c Z_t$; $X_t, Z_c$ fixed |

**Gottesman-Knill**: circuits of Clifford gates + Pauli measurements on stabilizer inputs are simulable in poly time — update $2n$ Pauli generators (a binary matrix) instead of $2^n$ amplitudes. **Entanglement alone is therefore not quantum advantage**: Bell states, GHZ, and stabilizer QEC are all Clifford.

**What it misses**: $T = \mathrm{diag}(1, e^{i\pi/4})$. $TXT^\dagger = (X+Y)/\sqrt2$ — not a Pauli, so $T \notin$ Clifford. Adding it gives universality; the quantumness beyond Clifford is called magic.

**Lab relevance**: averaging over random Cliffords twirls arbitrary noise into a depolarizing channel (the Clifford group is a unitary 2-design) — the engine under randomized benchmarking.

> [!question]- Clifford circuits generate massive entanglement — why are they still simulable?
> A stabilizer state, however entangled, is specified by $n$ Pauli generators ($\sim 2n^2$ bits), not $2^n$ amplitudes. Simulability tracks description size, not entanglement.
